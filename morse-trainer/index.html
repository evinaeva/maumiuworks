Features

Memorizing dots and dashes from a chart stalls fast — your ear needs the sound. Morsenta teaches Morse code by ear, one character at a time, the way radio clubs do: a complete CW trainer and Morse code translator, from the first lesson to a full contact (QSO). No ads, no account, no internet needed.

LEARN BY EAR WITH THE KOCH METHOD

  • Start with just two letters, K and M. Every time you get 90% right, a new character is unlocked.
  • Characters are played at real speed (20 WPM by default) with Farnsworth spacing, so you learn the sound of each letter instead of counting dots and dashes.
  • Tap what you hear and get instant feedback: what you chose, what it was, and the rhythm of the right answer.
  • Learn by sound, light or vibration: a signal lamp on the screen blinks the code, and the phone can vibrate it for silent practice.
  • Weak characters come back more often. Spaced review brings back the letters you are about to forget.
  • Already know some Morse? Choose where to start.

PRACTICE THAT GETS YOU ON THE AIR

  • Letter groups from the characters you know.
  • Common words — only with the letters you have already learned.
  • CW abbreviations (CQ, TNX, RST, 73…) and Q codes with their meaning.
  • Realistic call signs from around the world and number exchanges (599, serial numbers).
  • A full contact (QSO), line by line: call, report, name, location, goodbye.
  • Listen-only mode for head copy: understand Morse without writing it down.

TRAIN YOUR SPEED AND YOUR WEAK SPOTS

  • 60-second sprint: as many characters as you can, with a personal best.
  • Speed ladder: five right in a row and the speed goes up; a mistake slows it down.
  • Fix my mistakes: a lesson made only of the characters you mix up.
  • Challenges with crowns between lessons.
  • Listening without taps: hear it, say it in your head, then hear the answer. Or answer by voice.
  • On-air sound: hiss and fading, like a real radio.

SEND WITH THE ON-SCREEN KEY

  • Straight key or iambic paddles.
  • Live decoding of what you send, with your speed in WPM.
  • A clear rhythm report: dah length compared with a dit, pauses inside and between letters, and simple tips like "hold dahs a little longer".
  • Timing is measured from your touches, not from the sound, so headphone delay never changes your score.

MORSE CODE TRANSLATOR

  • Text to Morse and Morse to text, with a quick dot/dash keypad.
  • Play the code as sound, flash it on the screen or with the flashlight, or feel it as vibration.
  • Prosigns AR, SK, BT, KN and SOS.
  • Extra letters: Ä Ö Ü, Ñ, accented letters, Cyrillic and Wabun (kana).
  • Decode Morse with the microphone (beta): hold your phone near a radio or another phone and watch the text appear. Sound is analysed on the phone only.
  • Read a blinking lamp or flashlight with the camera (beta) and keep saved messages and recent translations.

CLEAN SOUND, YOUR SETTINGS

  • Smooth tone without key clicks, from 400 to 1000 Hz.
  • Separate character speed and overall speed (Farnsworth), 5–40 WPM.
  • A short silence before each message, so Bluetooth headphones do not cut the first dit.

PROGRESS YOU CAN SEE

  • Daily goal and streak — with no penalty for a missed day.
  • Accuracy and reaction time for every character, and your most frequent mix-ups (like U and V).
  • Practice history for the last 14 days.
  • Save a backup file and move your progress to a new phone.

Made for beginners, future radio amateurs preparing for CW, scouts, students and anyone curious about Morse code. Light and dark theme, larger text option, screen reader labels.

Lessons, practice and the translator work offline and without registration. Your progress stays on your phone.

Questions and answers

How long does it take to learn Morse code?

There is no fixed time: it depends mostly on how regularly you practice. The Morsenta course covers 40 characters: letters, digits and common signs. You start with just K and M, and a new character unlocks once you get at least 90% right over at least 20 answers. The app suggests ten minutes a day rather than an hour once a week, with a daily goal and a streak that has no penalty for a missed day.

What is the Koch method for learning Morse code?

The Koch method teaches Morse by ear at full character speed from the first lesson. You begin with two characters and add one more each time you copy at least 90% correctly, so you never count dots and dashes. Morsenta follows this method and plays characters at 20 WPM by default with Farnsworth spacing: each letter keeps its real rhythm while the gaps between letters are longer. Characters you often miss come back more often.

Do I need Morse code for the ham radio exam?

No. In the United States, amateur radio license exams have had no Morse code test since 2007. Many hams still learn CW to operate on the air, and that is what Morsenta trains: copying by ear, CW abbreviations and Q codes, call signs, signal reports and a full QSO line by line, plus an on-screen straight key or iambic paddles. The app does not include question pools for the written exams.

Can I practice Morse code offline?

Yes. Lessons, practice, the on-screen key, the translator and decoding all work without an internet connection, and there is no account or sign-in. Your progress stays on your phone, and a backup file lets you move it to a new one. When online, the app sends anonymous daily usage counts, which you can turn off in Settings. Answering by voice uses an online speech service only if you agree. No ads.

Can the app decode Morse code from sound or a flashlight?

Yes, with two beta features in the translator. Listen uses the microphone to decode a clear tone from a radio or another phone; noise, fading and several signals at once make it less reliable. Light uses the camera to read a blinking lamp or flashlight and works most reliably in a dark room at up to 12 WPM. Sound and camera frames are analyzed on your phone only and are never recorded or sent.

How do I translate text to Morse code?

Open the Translate tab and type your text, or tap dots and dashes on the keypad to turn Morse back into text. The code can be played as sound, flashed on the screen or with the phone's flashlight, or felt as vibration, and you can keep saved messages. Prosigns AR, SK, BT, KN and SOS are supported, as are extra letters such as Ä Ö Ü, Ñ, accented letters, Cyrillic and Japanese Wabun.
