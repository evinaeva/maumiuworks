Features

Memorising dots and dashes from a chart stalls fast — your ear needs the sound. Morsenta teaches Morse code by ear, one character at a time, the way radio clubs do: a complete CW trainer and Morse code translator, from the first lesson to a full contact (QSO). No ads, no account, no internet needed.

LEARN BY EAR WITH THE KOCH METHOD

  • Start with just two letters, K and M. Every time you get 90% right, a new character is unlocked.
  • Characters are played at real speed (20 WPM by default) with Farnsworth spacing, so you learn the sound of each letter instead of counting dots and dashes.
  • Tap what you hear and get instant feedback: what you chose, what it was, and the rhythm of the right answer.
  • Learn by sound, light or vibration: a signal lamp on the screen blinks the code, and the phone can vibrate it for silent practice.
  • Weak characters come back more often. Spaced review brings back the letters you are about to forget.
  • Already know some Morse? Choose where to start.

PRACTICE THAT GETS YOU ON THE AIR

  • Letter groups from the characters you know.
  • Common words — only with the letters you have already learnt.
  • CW abbreviations (CQ, TNX, RST, 73…) and Q codes with their meaning.
  • Realistic call signs from around the world and number exchanges (599, serial numbers).
  • A full contact (QSO), line by line: call, report, name, location, goodbye.
  • Listen-only mode for head copy: understand Morse without writing it down.

TRAIN YOUR SPEED AND YOUR WEAK SPOTS

  • 60-second sprint: as many characters as you can, with a personal best.
  • Speed ladder: five right in a row and the speed goes up; a mistake slows it down.
  • Fix my mistakes: a lesson made only of the characters you mix up.
  • Challenges with crowns between lessons.
  • Listening without taps: hear it, say it in your head, then hear the answer. Or answer by voice.
  • On-air sound: hiss and fading, like a real radio.

SEND WITH THE ON-SCREEN KEY

  • Straight key or iambic paddles.
  • Live decoding of what you send, with your speed in WPM.
  • A clear rhythm report: dah length compared with a dit, pauses inside and between letters, and simple tips like "hold dahs a little longer".
  • Timing is measured from your touches, not from the sound, so headphone delay never changes your score.

MORSE CODE TRANSLATOR AND DECODER

  • Text to Morse and Morse to text, with a quick dot/dash keypad.
  • Play the code as sound, flash it on the screen or with the torch, or feel it as vibration.
  • Prosigns AR, SK, BT, KN and SOS.
  • Extra letters: Ä Ö Ü, Ñ, accented letters, Cyrillic and Wabun (kana).
  • Decode Morse with the microphone (beta): hold your phone near a radio or another phone and watch the text appear. Sound is analysed on the phone only.
  • Read a blinking lamp or flashlight with the camera (beta) and keep saved messages and recent translations.

CLEAN SOUND, YOUR SETTINGS

  • Smooth tone without key clicks, from 400 to 1000 Hz.
  • Separate character speed and overall speed (Farnsworth), 5–40 WPM.
  • A short silence before each message, so Bluetooth headphones do not cut the first dit.

PROGRESS YOU CAN SEE

  • Daily goal and streak — with no penalty for a missed day.
  • Accuracy and reaction time for every character, and your most frequent mix-ups (like U and V).
  • Practice history for the last 14 days.
  • Save a backup file and move your progress to a new phone.

Made for beginners, future radio amateurs preparing for CW, scouts, students and anyone curious about Morse code. Light and dark theme, larger text option, screen reader labels.

Lessons, practice and the translator work offline and without registration. Your progress stays on your phone.

Questions and answers

How long does it take to learn Morse code?

Regular practice matters far more than talent, so there is no honest fixed figure. The Morsenta course has 40 characters: letters, digits and common signs. You start with K and M, and the next character is unlocked once you get at least 90% right over at least 20 answers. The app suggests ten minutes a day rather than an hour once a week, and its daily goal and streak never punish a missed day.

How does the Koch method work?

You learn every character by ear at full speed from the very first lesson, starting with just two. Once you copy them at 90% or more, one more is added, so each letter becomes a familiar sound instead of a pattern of dots and dashes to count. Morsenta plays characters at 20 WPM by default with Farnsworth spacing, and the characters you tend to get wrong come back more often until they stick.

Do I need Morse code for the amateur radio licence in the UK?

No. Morse has not been required for the UK amateur licence since 2003, yet many radio amateurs still learn CW to work stations on the air. Morsenta trains exactly that: copying by ear, CW abbreviations and Q codes, call signs, signal reports and a complete contact line by line, plus an on-screen straight key or iambic paddles. It does not contain questions for the Foundation, Intermediate or Full exams.

Can I learn Morse code without an internet connection?

Yes. Lessons, practice, the sending key, the translator and the decoders all work offline, with no account and no sign-in. Your progress is stored on the phone, and a backup file lets you move it to a new one. When the phone is online, the app sends anonymous daily usage counts, which you can switch off in Settings, and answering by voice uses an online speech service only if you agree. No ads.

Can my phone decode Morse code from sound or a torch?

Yes, with two beta features in the translator. Listen uses the microphone to decode a clear tone from a radio or another phone; noise, fading or several signals at once make it less reliable. Light uses the camera to read a blinking lamp or torch and works most reliably in a dark room at up to 12 WPM. Everything is analysed on the phone, and nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

How do I use the Morse code translator?

Type text to see it in Morse, or tap dots and dashes on the keypad to turn Morse back into text. Messages can be played as sound, flashed on the screen or with the phone's torch, or felt as vibration, and you can keep the ones you need as saved messages. Prosigns AR, SK, BT, KN and SOS are included, along with Ä Ö Ü, Ñ, accented letters, Cyrillic and Japanese Wabun.
