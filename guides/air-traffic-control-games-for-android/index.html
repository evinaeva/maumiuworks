Air traffic control games on Android fall into three groups: arcade games where you draw a route with your finger and keep planes from colliding, radar simulators where you give headings, altitudes and speeds the way an approach controller does, and airport management or tycoon games where you build terminals and earn money more than you guide traffic. Pick by how much realism you want and how long you like to play: arcade games suit short sessions, radar sims reward patience and some aviation knowledge, and tycoon games are slow builders you return to over weeks.

Below is how the types differ, what real controllers do for comparison, and a list of games we found on Google Play when we checked on November 26, 2026. We make one of them, PlaneFlow, and it is listed as one entry among the others.

How close are these games to real air traffic control?

Real control is split into jobs. At a towered airport, ground control handles aircraft taxiing on the airport surface, while the tower's local control clears aircraft to take off and land on the runways, as the FAA's Aeronautical Information Manual describes. Farther out, an approach controller works a radar screen and gives pilots headings, altitudes and speeds to line them up for the runway. A game usually takes one of these jobs, or blends several into one screen.

So it helps to know what each game simulates:

  • Arcade route-drawing games compress tower and approach work into one finger gesture. They teach sequencing and spacing by feel, not procedures.
  • Radar sims such as Endless ATC or Approach Control: Remastered are closest to approach control. You need to think about turn radius, descent rate and minimum spacing.
  • Tower and ground games focus on runway and taxiway conflicts: who lands first, who waits at the hold line, which gate is open.

None of them is training for the real job, but the radar sims give a fair idea of the workload.

Which air traffic control games are on Google Play now?

Every game below had a live Google Play listing when we checked. Download counts and prices change, so check the listing before you buy. "Paid" means the game is bought up front, as a one-time purchase.

Arcade route-drawing games

  • Air Control 2 (Four Pixels Games): draw paths to the runways for planes, helicopters and zeppelins, with weather, radio-jam events and a co-op mode for two players on one tablet.
  • Air Control (Four Pixels Games): the original game of the series, paid, with four maps and a classic mode plus a puzzle mode.
  • Planes Control - (ATC) (Rarepixels - Indie Games): path drawing for planes and helicopters, with unexpected events that change the routine.
  • Mini Airways: Premium (Erabit Studios): the Android version of the Steam game, paid; draw flight paths into 18 maps based on real airports, with weather and VIP flights, plus a more forgiving level mode and automatic collision avoidance made for phones.
  • PlaneFlow: Airport Dispatcher (Maumiu Works, our game): an arcade game about the ground side of the airport. You draw a route to land each plane, send it through the services it needs (fuel, passenger boarding, repairs) and launch it again. More in the PlaneFlow section below.

Tower view and radar simulators

  • Airport Madness 3D: Volume 2 (Big Fat Simulations): a 3D tower view of eight real-world airports, including New York JFK, London City and Lukla; the developer says it was designed by real air traffic controllers.
  • Unmatched Air Traffic Control (Vector3D Studios): a busy airport where you guide planes through landing, parking and takeoff with simple controls.
  • Take Control of the Tower (Luchtverkeersleiding Nederland, the Dutch air navigation service provider): a tower game about taking off and landing planes safely, with levels and rankings.
  • Endless ATC (startgrid): a paid approach-radar sim at nine real airports, such as Amsterdam Schiphol, London Heathrow and Tokyo Haneda, with pilot voices, weather and altitude restrictions; it plays without an internet connection and is in English only. A Lite version, Endless ATC Lite, is also listed.
  • Approach Control: Remastered (Big Fat Simulations): a radar approach sim with a 3-second radar sweep, separation and wake turbulence rules and several approach sectors.

Airport management and tycoon games

  • World of Airports (Flyboys.Games): manage a 3D model of a real international airport, handle traffic and staff, and receive other players' aircraft in multiplayer.
  • Airport City transport manager (Game Insight): a city builder with an airport at its center; ATC is a small part, building is the main part.
  • Airport Simulator: Plane City (Playrion): run terminals, flights and partner airlines at several airports.
  • Idle Airport Tycoon - Planes (Codigames): an idle game about growing an airport, buying planes and researching upgrades.
  • Jumbo Airport Story (Kairosoft): a paid pixel-art management game about building shops and facilities to make an airport popular.
  • Pocket Planes: Airline Tycoon (NimbleBit): an airline rather than an airport game: you buy planes and run their flights.

How do I choose the right ATC game?

  • Decide how real you want it. If radio calls, headings and altitudes sound fun, start with a radar sim. If you just want the "keep it all moving" feeling, an arcade game is the better fit.
  • Match it to your session length. Arcade games are built for quick rounds. Radar sims and tycoon games pay off in longer sessions.
  • Check the controls on your screen size. Drawing paths is comfortable on a phone. Dense radar screens are easier on a tablet.
  • Check offline needs. Some games need a connection for leaderboards or multiplayer only; the listing or the first launch in airplane mode will tell you.
  • Look at how it is sold. Paid games are a one-time purchase; others show "Contains ads" or "In-app purchases" on their Play listing. Read that line before you install.
  • Language. Some sims, such as Endless ATC, are in English only.

Where does PlaneFlow fit?

PlaneFlow is an arcade game, not a pilot game and not a radar sim. You play the airport's dispatcher: draw a route for each plane, land it, route it to a hangar for the jobs on its list, such as fuel, passenger boarding and repairs, and clear it for takeoff. Each serviced plane earns money, and a delayed flight pays less. During a level you can spend that money to open another hangar, buy an extra runway or upgrade a hangar so it works faster.

There are 16 campaign levels, each with its own goal and up to 3 stars. Rush hour brings an extra wave of arrivals, VIP flights pay more but are less patient, and medical aircraft need priority. On harder levels, up to a couple dozen planes can be in the air and on the field at once. After the campaign, survival mode is an endless run ranked on a global leaderboard, and seasonal leagues go from Bronze to Diamond. There are more than 30 medals plus Google Play Games achievements, and autosave and cloud saves keep your progress. Leaderboards and leagues need an internet connection; the campaign does not.

If you want the flow of an airport rather than the procedures of a radar room, it belongs on your shortlist next to Air Control 2 and Mini Airways. If you want headings and altitudes, choose Endless ATC or Approach Control: Remastered instead.

Questions and answers

What is the most realistic air traffic control game on Android?

The radar simulators are the most realistic. Endless ATC and Approach Control: Remastered have you assign headings, altitudes and speeds and keep aircraft separated, which is close to the work of an approach controller.

Are there air traffic control games that work offline?

Yes. Endless ATC states on its listing that it needs no internet connection, and PlaneFlow's campaign plays offline, with only leaderboards and leagues needing a connection. For other games, try launching them once in airplane mode.

Is PlaneFlow a flight simulator?

No. PlaneFlow is an arcade airport traffic control game. You never fly a plane; you draw routes to land planes, send them to hangars for fuel, boarding and repairs, and launch them again.

What is the difference between an ATC game and an airport tycoon game?

An ATC game is about guiding aircraft safely in real time: spacing, sequencing and avoiding collisions. A tycoon game is about building and earning: terminals, gates, flights and upgrades, often with only a simplified traffic role.
