To seed a bracket, rank the teams from strongest (seed 1) to weakest, then place them so that in every first-round game the two seeds add up to the bracket size plus one: in a 16-team bracket that is 1 vs 16, 8 vs 9, 4 vs 13, 5 vs 12, 2 vs 15, 7 vs 10, 3 vs 14 and 6 vs 11, with seeds 1 and 2 in opposite halves. If the number of teams is not a power of two, the number of byes is the next power of two minus the number of teams, and the byes go to the top seeds: 12 teams get 4 byes, for seeds 1 to 4.

What does seeding a tournament mean?

Seeding is ranking the teams before the draw and using that ranking to place them in the bracket. The goal is that the strongest teams cannot knock each other out early, so the later rounds are the most competitive and the final is, if results go as expected, between the two best teams. It also rewards past results: a team that earned a high ranking gets an easier path.

How do you decide the seeds?

Use the most objective ranking you have, in roughly this order of preference:

  1. An official ranking or rating (league standings, federation ranking, chess or Elo rating).
  2. Results from an earlier stage of the same event, such as group standings.
  3. Last season's or last edition's results.
  4. The organizer's judgment, announced before the draw.
  5. A random draw, if nobody knows who is strong.

Announce the method before you publish the bracket. Seeding by judgment is the part teams are most likely to argue about.

What is the standard seeding order, and why does it work?

The standard order is built so the favorites meet as late as possible. In each round, if the higher seed always wins, the seeds in each game add up to a fixed number:

Round (16 teams)Seeds add up toGames if favorites win
Round of 16171-16, 8-9, 4-13, 5-12, 2-15, 7-10, 3-14, 6-11
Quarterfinals91-8, 4-5, 2-7, 3-6
Semifinals51-4, 2-3
Final31-2

Reading the bracket from top to bottom, the top half holds seeds 1, 16, 8, 9, 4, 13, 5 and 12; the bottom half holds 2, 15, 7, 10, 3, 14, 6 and 11. This guarantees:

  • Seeds 1 and 2 can only meet in the final.
  • Seeds 1 to 4 can only meet in the semifinals or later.
  • Seeds 1 to 8 can only meet in the quarterfinals or later.

You can build the order for any power of two by doubling. Start with 1 vs 2. To double the bracket, replace each seed s with the pair "s vs (new size + 1 - s)": for 4 teams 1-4, 2-3; for 8 teams 1-8, 4-5, 2-7, 3-6; and so on. Some brackets (for example NCAA basketball) list the same first-round pairs in a different visual order, but the pairs and halves work the same way.

How many byes does a bracket need?

A knockout bracket needs a power of two in every round (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64). If you have N teams, round the bracket up to the next power of two, and the empty slots are byes:

A bye means a team goes straight to round 2. Put the byes where the lowest seeds would be (seed 16 in a 16-slot bracket, then 15, 14…), so seed 1 faces the empty slot, then seed 2, and so on. That way byes reward the top seeds, and no two byes meet, which would create an empty game. After round 1 the bracket has exactly a power of two teams left.

Example: how do you seed a 12-team bracket?

12 teams, bracket of 16, 4 byes for seeds 1 to 4. Use the 16-team order and leave out seeds 13 to 16:

Round 1Round 2
1 (bye)1 vs winner of 8-9
8 vs 9
4 (bye)4 vs winner of 5-12
5 vs 12
2 (bye)2 vs winner of 7-10
7 vs 10
3 (bye)3 vs winner of 6-11
6 vs 11

Round 1 has 4 games; round 2 is a normal 8-team quarterfinal. If the favorites keep winning, the semifinals are 1 vs 4 and 2 vs 3. The tournament has 11 games in total.

Example: how do you seed a 6-team bracket?

6 teams, bracket of 8, 2 byes for seeds 1 and 2.

  • Round 1: 4 vs 5 and 3 vs 6. Seeds 1 and 2 have byes.
  • Semifinals: 1 vs winner of 4-5, and 2 vs winner of 3-6.
  • Final: the two semifinal winners.

That is 5 games over 3 rounds. If the 2-game first round feels thin, the alternative is two groups of 3 (3 games each) with a final between the group winners, which gives every team at least 2 games.

A common middle ground is partial seeding: seed only the top 2, 4 or 8 and draw everyone else at random into the remaining slots. Tennis Grand Slams, for example, seed 32 of the 128 players in the singles draw. When you draw seeds 3 and 4 at random into the two remaining quarters (and seeds 5 to 8 into the remaining eighths), the protection still holds while the bracket varies from year to year.

How do you protect top seeds and keep teams apart?

  • Separate the top two. Seed 1 at the top of the bracket, seed 2 at the bottom.
  • Separate teams from the same club, school or group. Put them in different halves or quarters so they cannot meet in round 1. In groups followed by playoffs, pair a group winner with a runner-up from another group.
  • Byes to the top. Never give byes at random; a lucky bye for a weak team is the most visible unfairness in a bracket.
  • Do not reseed silently. Some events reseed after each round (the highest remaining seed plays the lowest). That is a valid rule, but it must be announced before the first game.

How do you seed groups for groups and playoffs?

Use snake seeding, also called serpentine. With 4 groups: seeds 1, 2, 3 and 4 go to groups A, B, C, D; seeds 5, 6, 7, 8 go to D, C, B, A; seeds 9 to 12 go to A, B, C, D again, and so on. Group A then has seeds 1, 8, 9 and 16, group B has 2, 7, 10 and 15. Each group has a similar total strength.

Questions and answers

Who does the number 1 seed play in the first round?

The lowest seed: seed 16 in a 16-team bracket, seed 8 in an 8-team bracket. If the bracket has byes, seed 1 gets the first bye and plays the winner of the 8 vs 9 game (16-slot bracket) in round 2.

How many byes are in a 12-team bracket?

4 byes: the next power of two is 16, and 16 - 12 = 4. Seeds 1, 2, 3 and 4 get them.

How many byes are in a 10-team bracket?

6 byes: 16 - 10 = 6. Seeds 1 to 6 go straight to the quarterfinals, and seeds 7 to 10 play two first-round games, 7 vs 10 and 8 vs 9.

Why is 8 vs 9 next to 1 vs 16 in a bracket?

Because the winner plays seed 1 in round 2. The 8-9 game is the closest first-round match, and its winner is the expected quarterfinal opponent of the top seed. Seed 2 gets the 7-10 winner, seed 3 the 6-11 winner and seed 4 the 5-12 winner.

Is random seeding fair?

It is fair in the sense that everyone has the same chances before the draw, but it can put the two strongest teams in the same first-round game. Use it when you have no reliable ranking, or seed only the top few and draw the rest.
