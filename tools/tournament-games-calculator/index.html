Why a knockout has N − 1 games

Every game knocks out exactly one team, and every team except the champion has to be knocked out once. So 16 teams play 15 games, and 100 teams play 99, however the byes fall. When N is not a power of two the bracket is filled up to the next one with byes for the top seeds: byes = next power of two − N, so 12 teams get 4 byes.

Why double elimination has 2N − 2 or 2N − 1 games

Everyone except the champion is out after exactly two losses: 2(N − 1) losses, one per game. The champion may also have lost once: if the losers-bracket champion wins the grand final, a reset final is played, which makes 2N − 1.

Why a round robin has N(N − 1) ÷ 2 games

Each of the N teams meets the other N − 1, and every game involves two teams, so the count is halved: 8 teams play 8 × 7 ÷ 2 = 28 games. With an even number, N ÷ 2 games fit into each round, so it takes N − 1 rounds. With an odd number one team rests each round, so it takes N rounds.

Groups + playoff

Teams are spread over G groups by snake seeding (1, 2, 3, 4 into groups A, B, C, D, then 5, 6, 7, 8 into D, C, B, A, and so on), each group plays a round robin, and the top q of each group go into a knockout. Example: 16 teams in 4 groups of 4, top 2 through: 4 × 6 = 24 group games plus 8 − 1 = 7 playoff games, 31 in total.

Swiss system

Everyone plays every round against someone with the same or a similar score, without rematches. ⌈log₂ N⌉ rounds are usually enough to leave a single player with a perfect score, so 16 players play 4 rounds of 8 games. With an odd N one player has a bye each round, which counts as a win.

How the time is estimated

The matches of one round share the courts, and the next round starts when that round is over: in a knockout or a Swiss round the pairings depend on the results, and in a league it keeps everyone on the same round. A round of 8 games on 3 courts needs ⌈8 ÷ 3⌉ = 3 time slots. Total time = time slots × minutes per match. In double elimination the winners and losers rounds are counted one after another. Breaks are not counted, so plan a little extra.

Questions and answers

How many games are in a tournament with 16 teams?

Single elimination: 15 games in 4 rounds. Double elimination: 30 games, or 31 with a grand final reset. Round robin: 120 games in 15 rounds (240 with home and away). 4 groups of 4 with the top 2 in a playoff: 24 group games plus 7 playoff games, 31 in total. Swiss: 4 rounds of 8 games, 32 games.

How many rounds does a knockout tournament have?

⌈log₂ N⌉, the number of times you can halve the field: 8 teams need 3 rounds, 16 need 4, 32 need 5. If N is not a power of two, round up: 12 or 13 teams still need 4 rounds, and the top seeds get byes in round 1.

Which format has the fewest games?

Single elimination, with N − 1 games, but half the teams play only once. Swiss gives everyone the same number of games (about log₂ N) with far fewer games than a round robin. Double elimination guarantees at least 2 games each for about twice the knockout games. A round robin is the fairest and the longest: N(N − 1) ÷ 2 games.

How many Swiss rounds should I play?

The usual number is ⌈log₂ N⌉: 3 rounds for 8 players, 4 for 16, 5 for 32, 7 for 100. That is enough rounds to separate the top players. Fewer rounds leave more players tied, more rounds make it closer to a round robin. It can never be more than N − 1.

How long will my tournament take?

Count time slots: the matches of one round are spread over your courts, so a round of 8 games on 3 courts takes ⌈8 ÷ 3⌉ = 3 slots, and the next round starts after that. Total time = time slots × minutes per match. Add breaks and changeovers on top; the calculator does not include them.
